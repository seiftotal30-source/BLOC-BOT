ESC bench test circuit

The ESP32 drives the brushed ESC with a servo pulse on one wire. It takes only signal and ground from the ESC's thin lead. The thin red 5 V wire stays unconnected because the buck already powers the ESP32.

positive ground (all tied together) signal, thin servo lead not connected
Check before plugging in: both thick pairs on this ESC end in red JST plugs. Confirm on the board's printed labels or the seller listing which pair is battery and which is motor. Battery on the motor output can kill the ESC.

Test order

  1. Lift the wheel off the table.
  2. Power the supply with the ESC switched off. The ESP32 boots from the buck.
  3. Upload the test sketch. It sends 1500 µs (neutral) first.
  4. Switch the ESC on. Wait for the arming beeps.
  5. Watch for forward, stop, reverse, stop.
  6. No beeps or no movement: measure white-to-black on the signal lead. About 0.25 V DC means the pulse is there.

Pulse widths

PulseMotor
1500 µsStop (neutral, arm here)
1650 µsSlow forward
1350 µsSlow reverse, if the ESC is bidirectional
1150–1850 µsSafe range. Past it the TT motor gets more than about 6 V.

If it only arms at 1000 µs, it's a forward-only ESC: 1000 µs is stop, 1000–1700 µs is forward.
